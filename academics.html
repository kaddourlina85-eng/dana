Her journey

What comes next

Graduate studies, wrongful-conviction research, and the possibility of teaching one day.

Dana grew up in Syria, where the sound of gunfire and drones overhead were part of daily life, and where her father had to leave the country to provide for the family. A decade later, she graduated from a Canadian university with honours.

“The community and the professors were so supportive — I met my best friends here.”

Before law, Dana considered acting. She chose a different path for the stability it could offer her family: her father, once a doctor, and her mother, once a teacher, gave up their careers to bring the family to Canada.

“It’s our turn.”

Wrongful convictions

The subject that changed the direction of her career.

Dana became deeply interested in the human cost of wrongful convictions, especially the ways they affect women, families, and trust in the justice system.
